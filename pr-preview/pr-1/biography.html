Hello all would-be adventurers,

My name is Harry aka Game Master Rhuyee! I am a professional Game Master (GM) and Storyteller here to guide you on your next thrilling adventure! Whether you're a brand-new player looking for a safe environment to explore a new hobby, a seasoned player looking to give your forever GM a break, or someone looking to host a memorable team building/community engagement experience, I'm excited to work with you to create experiences you'll remember for years to come.

What is a Tabletop Role Playing Game?

For those that are new to the very concept of Tabletop Role-Playing Games (TTRPGs), they are collaborative storytelling engines where (in most cases) players are each piloting the fate of their very own custom character, while the GM is weaving together the surrounding world, challenges, and events that they must encounter on their journey. The GM, also called a Dungeon Master (DM) in some game systems, uses dice and other game mechanics to facilitate the outcomes and the subsequent impacts of each player's decisions on the ever-evolving world around them.

Each table's story is their own personal sandbox based entirely around their unique decisions changing the path their story will take. TTRPGs are an amazing outlet for channeling creative passions and for generating a vast library of inside jokes with your table. In a world where stories are increasingly defined by rigid formulas and tropes, TTRPGs are your way of building the stories you want to see present in the world.

But beyond simply being a fun creative outlet, there is a growing body of emerging studies indicating the vast therapeutic potential of TTRPGs with respect to both psychosocial and social skills. While engaging with TTRPGs, players are constantly reminded, by challenges too great for any one person to succeed on their own, that sometimes it takes a village to succeed. Players learn how to improve their ability to communicate and understand each other as they work together towards a common end.

Unlike other team building exercises or self-help books where the information is simply heaped at the individual with no practical opportunities to engage with the lessons, TTRPGs enrapture each player in actively practicing personal development and communication without even realizing it. The skills refined at the table are carried over to our real lives improving how we navigate our interpersonal relationships, our ability to empathize, and our capacity for self-reflection. If you're looking to find new ways of connecting with your friends or are planning a team building event that's more than a speaker scrolling through a slide deck, TTRPGs will foster real retained growth in an engaging fun atmosphere.

What tabletop games mean to me personally:

Now that I've hooked you on TTRPGs being the greatest thing ever, I should tell you a little bit about who I am. Back when I was a little kid, I already loved sharing stories. I remember using crayons and paperclips to make books that my teachers would humor me and let me read aloud to my class. Then as I got older, I had trouble finding an outlet for sharing all of the rich narratives I had strung together in my daydreams.

All those rich stories would stay locked up in my head until college when a friend invited me to play in their game of Dungeons and Dragons. It wasn't long before I was hooked and couldn't wait for my next session. I became so impatient for the next chance to play that I started up my own game. Since then I've played every Sunday like clockwork. Getting to spend hours with my closest friends springboarding the wildest ideas off each other into an ever-spiraling story was a dream come true and I've had the privilege of getting to keep living that dream ever since.

For me, TTRPGs have been at the core of my strongest relationships. Most of the cherished loved ones that joined me in celebrating my wedding were all players at my table at one point or another. The bonds we made playing TTRPGs have evolved into lifelong connections that stretch beyond the table. I have been so honored all these years to enjoy their creativity and will forever remember the acclamation that rang out across the hall at the end of the wedding reception: Three cheers for the Dungeon Master.

My tabletop experience and style.

I have successfully run well over a dozen campaigns, typically running sessions routinely on a weekly or monthly schedule. I've juggled managing multiple separate campaigns at the same time while seamlessly weaving in innumerable one-shots for holidays, special occasions, or to try out a fresh-on-the-shelves system. Even though I had my start with Dungeons and Dragons 5th edition, I've since diversified out into a host of other systems ranging from well-known names, including Daggerheart and Vampire the Masquerade, to indie darlings like Minimum Rage and Eat the Reich.

My approach to running a table is to always create a compelling narrative where every player feels a sense of autonomy and recognizes that their voice is heard. Regardless of your background, lifestyle, or experiences, you belong at the table and this story belongs to all of us. Bigotry, hate, and generative AI have absolutely no place at my table. Human connection and creativity are irreplaceable.

My strengths as a GM are often described by my players as a near encyclopedic knowledge of rules, rich engaging lore, personalized engagement, and willingness to bend to rule of cool. My stories often engage with narratives of found family using their cleverness to triumph over adversity to provide catharsis for the powerlessness we may experience in our everyday lives. When exploring heavy themes I utilize curated TTRPG safety tools to mitigate any stress players may experience.

Stories from my table.

The best way to gain insights into the diversity of games I've run at my table and am willing to host is by offering a glance at the games I've previously run:

Outlaws vs Illithids

In a wild west inspired setting, a band of outlaws stumble their way into a murder mystery involving wealthy socialites aboard a locomotive roaring its way across frontier lands. Their ensuing adventures into the dark underbelly of corporate succession wars pit them against Ducanon Dimmagnome, a ruthless capitalist owner of the gladiatorial Dimma-Blood-dome, as he obsessively endeavors to unearth otherworldly relics best left buried in the sands.

The Whole Grain Gang

In this setting, based loosely on Ancient Greece, the death of a god carries lasting impacts that threaten to pit a pantheon against each other. Following the goddess Persephone's tragic passing, a party of formerly unremarkable villagers trying to survive harsh times receive divine boons to aid them in navigating lands forever changed by the brewing war between Hades and Demeter over who will gain custody of Persephone's child, the last gift she bestowed upon the world. Along the journey they must mediate a heated pie-baking rivalry, contend with carbon monoxide poisoning, and witness what has become of heroes after the stories end.

It May Be a Small World But It's Our World

In a post-apocalyptic worldscape where civilization has long since fallen to environmental decay, a bunker full of goblin survivors find that old debts their elders have kept hidden from them have come due. They are forced to leave the familiar caves that have always been their home and without any elders left to guide them, must chart their own course through a world retaken by nature and still haunted by the sins of those that came before.

The Lies on the Other Side of the Mirror

In a sleepy island town off the coast of the Pacific Northwest, one bad storm is all it really takes to dismiss the illusion that escape was ever an option. There are things lurking out there in the deep woods that are best left alone, and the person standing next to you may not be a person at all. What does it mean to be human? And to what lengths will you go to remain one? Trust no one, especially not the face in the mirror.
